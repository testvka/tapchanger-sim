1. Three-winding transformer: RatioTapChanger on end 1 + PhaseTapChangerTabular on end 2

This combination

Three-winding 220/110/20 kV transformer TX with a RatioTapChanger on end 1 (220 kV) and a PhaseTapChangerTabular on end 2 (110 kV).

Ratio and phase tap changers together: magnitude and angle change at the same time, on different ends.

This is configuration 1 of the 32 example configurations; it comes as a pair: TC01_3W_RTC1_PTCtab2 (with load) and NL01_3W_RTC1_PTCtab2 (no load).

What each tap changer does

  • RatioTapChanger on end 1 (220 kV), step 14 (neutral 11): ratio = 1 + (14 − 11) · 1.25 / 100 = 1.0375. A ratio tap changer scales the winding voltage in phase: t = 1 + n · stepVoltageIncrement / 100 with n = step − neutral; the angle stays 0°. On end 1, the 220 kV side fed by the grid, the voltage behind the tap changer is V / t, so it acts on both B2 and B3 against its own value: a ratio above 1 lowers it. Here it changes B2 by -4.066 kV and B3 by -0.739 kV.
  • PhaseTapChangerTabular on end 2 (110 kV), step 9 (neutral 7): table point 9: angle 5.5°, ratio 1.01. A tabular phase tap changer takes angle and ratio of every step from its table. The points need not be equidistant, so no formula converts a step into an angle. On end 2 (110 kV) it multiplies the voltage at B2 by t: a positive angle turns it forward and a ratio above 1 raises it; B3 does not see it. Here it changes B2 by +1.084 kV and +5.500°.

How they combine

Between the buses only the product of each end's tap changers counts, divided by end 1's: B1 → B2: t2 / t1 = 0.97349 at +5.500°; B1 → B3: t3 / t1 = 0.96386 at +0.000° (1 at 0° would be the plain transformer).

End 1 works against the other ends on the path from B1: the same value on end 1 and on end k cancels (t_k / t_1 = 1). A program that puts a tap changer on the wrong end therefore inverts its effect.

Three-winding transformer: end 1 is common to both paths, so its tap changers move B2 and B3 together; tap changers on end 2 or end 3 act only on their own bus.

With load and parallel TP (TC01_3W_RTC1_PTCtab2)

Load 160 MW / 50 Mvar at B2 and 30 MW / 10 Mvar at B3; the plain transformer TP (no tap changer) is in parallel with TX between B1 and B2.

Without tap changers TX would take 102.6 MW / 37.3 Mvar at B1 and TP 87.6 MW / 31.5 Mvar (TX also feeds B3, so the two do not share evenly even without taps). With these steps TX takes 199.0 MW / 15.7 Mvar and TP -8.5 MW / 64.0 Mvar.

So 96.4 MW and 21.7 Mvar circulate in the loop B1 – TX – B2 – TP: TX takes more active and less reactive power than without tap changers. The angle of t2 / t1 (+5.500°) drives the active part, its magnitude (0.97349) the reactive part: a phase shifter steers active power, a ratio tap changer reactive power. That is why a wrong angle or ratio in a program shows up as a wrong split between TX and TP.

No load (NL01_3W_RTC1_PTCtab2)

TX alone, energized from B1 at 225.0 kV with B2 and B3 open. No load current flows, so the open-circuit voltages show the tap configuration directly: V_k = V_B1 · (U_k / 220 kV) · t_k / t_1.

B2: 109.512 kV at +5.499° (the formula gives 109.518 kV at +5.500°; the small rest is the magnetizing current); B3: 19.714 kV at -0.001° (the formula gives 19.715 kV at +0.000°; the small rest is the magnetizing current).

Only the magnetizing branch on end 1 draws power: 0.0972 MW and 0.2226 Mvar. A phase shifter steers nothing here (no load, no parallel path): its angle appears directly as the angle of the open bus.

The pair: TC01_3W_RTC1_PTCtab2 / NL01_3W_RTC1_PTCtab2

Both cases have the same tap changers at the same steps. The no-load case isolates the tap changers: TX alone, the other ends open, so the open-circuit voltages are exactly the tap configuration. The loaded case adds the load and the parallel TP: the same configuration now shows as a circulating flow, where a wrong ratio or angle becomes many MW or Mvar.

At no load B2 is at 109.512 kV and +5.499°, with load at 109.210 kV and +0.273°: load and TP add -5.226° and -0.302 kV to what the tap changers alone give.

What a program reading this case must get right

  • RatioTapChanger on end 1: B2 and B3 get 1 / t = 0.96386, not t = 1.03750.
  • Tabular: take point 9 of the table (+5.5°, ratio 1.010); do not interpolate a linear law.
  • Sign on end 2: ENTSO-E's CGMES 3.0 data read the angle of a tabular phase tap changer on its own winding; ENTSO-E's CGMES 2.4.15 conformity cases read it inverted, seen from end 1. This page uses the own-winding reading: B2 is 109.210 kV at +0.273°; read the other way it would be 108.088 kV at -5.124°.
  • Three-winding transformer: every tap changer belongs to its own PowerTransformerEnd; attached to another end it would move the wrong bus.

31. Two-winding transformer: PhaseTapChangerLinear on end 1 + RatioTapChanger on end 2

This combination

Two-winding 220/110 kV transformer TX with a PhaseTapChangerLinear on end 1 (220 kV) and a RatioTapChanger on end 2 (110 kV).

Ratio and phase tap changers together: magnitude and angle change at the same time, on different ends.

This is configuration 31 of the 32 example configurations; it comes as a pair: TC31_2W_PTClin1_RTC2 (with load) and NL31_2W_PTClin1_RTC2 (no load).

What each tap changer does

  • PhaseTapChangerLinear on end 1 (220 kV), step 16 (neutral 13): angle = (16 − 13) · 1.5° = 4.500°. A linear phase tap changer turns the voltage by n · stepPhaseShiftIncrement and leaves the magnitude at 1. CGMES describes it only by the angle per step, not by a winding design. On end 1, the 220 kV side fed by the grid, the voltage behind the tap changer is V / t, so it acts on B2 against its own value: a positive angle turns it back. Here it changes B2 by +0.000 kV and -4.500°.
  • RatioTapChanger on end 2 (110 kV), step 6 (neutral 9): ratio = 1 + (6 − 9) · 1.5 / 100 = 0.9550. A ratio tap changer scales the winding voltage in phase: t = 1 + n · stepVoltageIncrement / 100 with n = step − neutral; the angle stays 0°. On end 2 (110 kV) it multiplies the voltage at B2 by t: a ratio above 1 raises it. Here it changes B2 by -5.063 kV.

How they combine

Between the buses only the product of each end's tap changers counts, divided by end 1's: B1 → B2: t2 / t1 = 0.95500 at -4.500° (1 at 0° would be the plain transformer).

End 1 works against the other ends on the path from B1: the same value on end 1 and on end k cancels (t_k / t_1 = 1). A program that puts a tap changer on the wrong end therefore inverts its effect.

With load and parallel TP (TC31_2W_PTClin1_RTC2)

Load 200 MW / 60 Mvar at B2; the plain transformer TP (no tap changer) is in parallel with TX between B1 and B2.

Without tap changers TX would take 100.1 MW / 35.2 Mvar at B1 and TP 100.1 MW / 35.2 Mvar (two equal transformers share the load evenly). With these steps TX takes 17.3 MW / -11.4 Mvar and TP 183.2 MW / 90.8 Mvar.

So 82.8 MW and 46.6 Mvar circulate in the loop B1 – TX – B2 – TP: TX takes less active and less reactive power than without tap changers. The angle of t2 / t1 (-4.500°) drives the active part, its magnitude (0.95500) the reactive part: a phase shifter steers active power, a ratio tap changer reactive power. That is why a wrong angle or ratio in a program shows up as a wrong split between TX and TP.

No load (NL31_2W_PTClin1_RTC2)

TX alone, energized from B1 at 225.0 kV with B2 open. No load current flows, so the open-circuit voltages show the tap configuration directly: V_k = V_B1 · (U_k / 220 kV) · t_k / t_1.

B2: 107.432 kV at -4.501° (the formula gives 107.438 kV at -4.500°; the small rest is the magnetizing current).

Only the magnetizing branch on end 1 draws power: 0.1046 MW and 0.2397 Mvar. A phase shifter steers nothing here (no load, no parallel path): its angle appears directly as the angle of the open bus.

The pair: TC31_2W_PTClin1_RTC2 / NL31_2W_PTClin1_RTC2

Both cases have the same tap changers at the same steps. The no-load case isolates the tap changers: TX alone, the other ends open, so the open-circuit voltages are exactly the tap configuration. The loaded case adds the load and the parallel TP: the same configuration now shows as a circulating flow, where a wrong ratio or angle becomes many MW or Mvar.

At no load B2 is at 107.432 kV and -4.501°, with load at 107.982 kV and -4.960°: load and TP add -0.459° and +0.551 kV to what the tap changers alone give.
